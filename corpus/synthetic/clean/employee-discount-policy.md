# Employee Discount Policy

**Policy ID:** STAFF-POL-002 · **Effective:** January 1, 2026

## 1. Standard Discount

Active employees receive a 20% discount on personal purchases, applied at
checkout using the employee's staff ID rather than a code. The discount
applies to the pre-tax price and is visible as a separate line item on the
receipt for audit purposes.

## 2. Interaction with Loyalty Points

Purchases made using the employee discount do not accrue loyalty points,
per LOY-POL-001 Section 2 — the discounted price does not reflect standard
program economics, and allowing accrual on top of an already-discounted
purchase was identified during the loyalty program's design as a
double-benefit case worth explicitly excluding rather than leaving
ambiguous.

## 3. Interaction with Markdown/Clearance Pricing

This is the section employees ask about most often, and the answer is not
intuitive: the employee discount **does** stack with clearance/markdown
pricing under the Markdown Policy (PROMO-POL-003), applied after the
markdown price, not the original tag price. A $100 item marked down 30% to
$70 receives the employee discount on the $70 price, for a final price of
$56 — not 50% off the original $100. This differs from the customer-facing
Discount Stacking Rules (PROMO-POL-002), which caps combined discounts at
50% of original price; the employee discount is not subject to that cap
because it is a staff benefit rather than a customer promotion, and the two
policies deliberately do not cross-apply their limits to each other.

## 4. Purchase Limits

Employee-discounted purchases are capped at $2,000 per rolling 30-day
period. Purchases beyond this cap are still permitted but do not receive
the discount on the portion exceeding $2,000 — the cap applies per
transaction total against the rolling window, not as a hard block.

## 5. Returns on Employee-Discounted Purchases

Items purchased with the employee discount follow the same return windows
as RET-POL-001, with one difference: the refund amount is calculated on
the discounted price actually paid, not the original tag price, consistent
with how any promotional-price return is handled.

## 6. Discount Misuse

Using the employee discount on behalf of a non-employee, or applying it to
a purchase intended for resale, is treated as a policy violation subject to
disciplinary review, up to and including termination for repeated or
large-value cases. This is distinct from, and handled separately from, the
till-discrepancy escalation path in SOP-CASH-009 — discount misuse is a
policy-compliance issue, not a cash-handling discrepancy, even though both
can surface during the same shift.
